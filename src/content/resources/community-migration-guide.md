Migration is a community change program supported by technology. Use these stages to avoid treating it as a bulk import.

## Stage 1: purpose and sponsorship

Document why change is needed, member benefits, trade-offs, accountable sponsor and success outcomes. State which current channels remain.

## Stage 2: community research

Interview active, quiet and former members. Observe tasks. Include accessibility, language, device and connectivity needs. Identify informal leaders and existing support practices.

## Stage 3: information inventory

Classify maintained content, formal records, useful history, duplicates, sensitive material and items that cannot move. Assign owners and review dates. Approve deletion rather than copying clutter.

## Stage 4: governance

Confirm charter, membership, roles, standards, moderation, privacy, retention, support and service-exit responsibilities before configuring access.

## Stage 5: technical trial

Run a protected sample export and import. Check authorship, dates, relationships, files, groups, permissions and search. Delete temporary copies after approved validation.

## Stage 6: representative pilot

Test complete journeys across member and administrator roles. Include invitation, sign-in, mobile, assistive technology, notifications, error recovery, reporting and departure.

## Stage 7: onboarding

Explain purpose, channel changes, privacy, standards and help. Focus the first visit on a useful task. Train administrators and moderators with scenarios.

## Stage 8: managed overlap

Set a clear period when old and new channels coexist. Direct maintained information and new community activity to the portal without duplicating everything indefinitely.

## Stage 9: closure

Complete records capture and final export. Communicate access changes. Revoke privileged accounts and integrations. Apply approved archive and deletion decisions.

## Stage 10: learning

Review search gaps, recurring support, accessibility barriers, unused privileged access and member outcomes. Fix governance and content as well as product issues.
