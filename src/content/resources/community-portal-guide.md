A community portal can become the stable digital home for an organisation and the people connected to it. This guide turns that ambition into a sequence of decisions.

## 1. Define the community

Write a one-page brief covering purpose, members, boundaries and expected outcomes. Name what the portal will not do. Identify public information that should remain on the open website and formal processes that belong elsewhere.

Describe member groups and access needs. Include people who may face disability, language, device, connectivity or confidence barriers.

**Output:** a community charter and member map.

## 2. Understand the present ecosystem

Inventory email lists, social groups, chat, websites, drives, event tools, identity sources and support systems. For each one, record its purpose, owner, audience, important content and known problems.

Do not assume every channel should disappear. Decide which system remains authoritative for public information, urgent communication, transactions and formal records.

**Output:** a channel map and agreed role for the portal.

## 3. Design community governance

Assign responsibility for membership, announcements, documents, groups, moderation, support, privacy, security and accessibility. Translate those responsibilities into least-privilege product roles.

Publish readable community standards. Define reports, escalation, serious harm, review and urgent channels. Establish content ownership and review dates.

**Output:** operating model, roles and standards.

## 4. Map information and privacy

List information categories, purposes, access, suppliers, locations, retention and deletion. Collect only what the purpose requires. Separate eligibility information from member-visible profiles.

Assess advertising, analytics, AI and integrations explicitly. Private community content should not become unrelated commercial profiling or automatic model-training material.

**Output:** data map, privacy assessment and retention plan.

## 5. Prioritise platform capability

Rank requirements as essential, important or optional. Use member scenarios rather than isolated feature names. A requirement such as “find the current building move guide on a phone” is more testable than “document management”.

Assess identity, permissions, announcements, discussions, knowledge, search, events, directories, support, moderation, accessibility, integration and export.

**Output:** prioritised, scenario-based requirements.

## 6. Verify trust claims

Review current security and accessibility evidence. Confirm processor and data-location details. Examine the agreement for privacy, AI, incident, export, deletion and service-exit terms.

Certifications are useful only within scope and date. Marketing language is not contractual evidence.

**Output:** assurance record, risks and contract commitments.

## 7. Prepare content and migration

Inventory current material. Move maintained, useful content; archive required records; delete unnecessary duplicates according to policy. Add titles, descriptions, owners and review dates.

Test export and import with real context. Protect migration copies and delete temporary files when finished.

**Output:** clean launch content and migration record.

## 8. Pilot with representative people

Test complete journeys across roles, devices and accessibility needs. Include invitation, sign-in, finding information, contributing, notifications, errors, moderation and departure.

Measure task understanding and outcomes. A pilot is not only a preference survey.

**Output:** prioritised fixes and launch decision.

## 9. Onboard and support

Explain why the portal exists, what moved, what remains elsewhere, how information is handled and where help is available. Keep the first visit focused on a useful task.

Train administrators with scenarios. Provide accessible alternatives for essential information and participation.

**Output:** member onboarding, administrator training and support plan.

## 10. Review responsibly

Review unresolved searches, recurring support, accessibility barriers, moderator workload, privileged access, inactive content and member feedback. Collect the least detailed data needed for each decision.

Set regular and change-triggered governance reviews. Plan service exit before it is needed.

**Output:** improvement rhythm and accountable owners.
