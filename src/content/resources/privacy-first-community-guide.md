A private login is not enough to make a community privacy-first. Privacy needs to shape the purpose, commercial model, product configuration and everyday governance.

## Purpose checklist

- Can members understand why the community exists?
- Is each information category connected to a stated task?
- Are unrelated future uses excluded unless separately assessed?
- Is public information kept public rather than unnecessarily gated?

## Collection checklist

- Is every required profile field genuinely required?
- Can eligibility be confirmed without storing the underlying detail?
- Are optional fields labelled and private by default where appropriate?
- Are precise location, demographic or behavioural fields avoided unless necessary?

## Audience and access checklist

- Can members see the audience before contributing?
- Are administrators divided into least-privilege roles?
- Are temporary and group roles reviewed?
- Do search, notifications and exports preserve permissions?

## Choice checklist

- Are privacy, directory and notification choices easy to find?
- Are alternatives presented without dark patterns?
- Is departure explained before confirmation?
- Is support available when a case requires human judgement?

## Commercial model checklist

- Who pays for the service?
- Does the provider sell personal information or behavioural advertising?
- Is community activity combined with cross-site profiles?
- Do contract terms match the public privacy position?

## Analytics checklist

- Which decision requires evidence?
- Can aggregate or qualitative information answer it?
- Who can access raw data and for how long?
- Could the measure become employee, resident or member surveillance?

## AI checklist

- What precise purpose does AI serve?
- Which content and provider are involved?
- Are prompts or outputs retained or used for training?
- What human review, correction and appeal exist?
- Can the organisation disable or limit the capability?

Portal Page's principle is that private community content should not automatically become training material. Any moderation assistance still needs documented controls.

## Retention checklist

- Does each information category have a trigger, period and disposal action?
- Are legal holds and formal records handled separately?
- Are dormant accounts and expired groups reviewed?
- Do backup and restoration processes respect deletion?

## Change checklist

- Do new features, integrations and processors receive privacy review?
- Are material changes communicated clearly?
- Can members raise concerns through an accountable route?
- Are policy, contract and product behaviour kept aligned?
