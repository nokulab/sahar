A community is more than the content stored in its current platform. It includes relationships, expectations, informal leaders, habits and a shared sense of place. Migration succeeds when it moves the purpose and supports the people—not merely when files appear in a new database.

## Explain why change is needed

Members need a reason that matters to them: easier access to current information, clearer privacy, better group structure, an organisation-owned environment or more useful support.

Be honest about trade-offs. A dedicated portal may require a new sign-in and new habits. It may not reproduce every feature. Do not manufacture urgency or imply that every member asked for the change.

Identify which existing channels will remain and what each will do after launch.

## Research the living community

Speak with active members, quiet members, moderators, administrators and people who left. Observe real tasks. Find the unofficial practices that keep the current community functioning.

Ask which history matters, who answers recurring questions, what notification patterns are tolerated and which barriers exclude people. Include disability, language, device and connectivity needs.

Map risks as well as desired features. Migration can expose old private material, re-notify members, reset visibility or give new administrators access.

## Inventory content and data

Classify current material:

- maintained information that should move;
- formal records that belong in an authoritative system;
- valuable discussion that needs context;
- duplicate or outdated material to remove;
- personal or sensitive material requiring special treatment;
- content that cannot be transferred under platform terms or law.

Do not import everything because an export exists. Migration is an opportunity to reduce unnecessary data and establish ownership and review dates.

## Establish governance before configuration

Confirm the charter, membership model, roles, standards, moderation, support and retention. Configure the portal around those decisions.

Test joiner, role-change and departure scenarios. Name the people responsible for publishing, group administration and member help. Prepare escalation for urgent safety, privacy or security concerns.

## Build and test a representative pilot

Choose a group that represents real complexity without creating uncontrolled risk. Include different roles and access needs. Use real but carefully selected content.

Test the complete experience from invitation through ordinary tasks. Check mobile widths, keyboard and assistive technology, notification content, errors and support. Run permission tests from each role rather than relying on administrator previews.

Collect feedback about understanding and outcomes, not only preference. Fix governance and content gaps as well as interface issues.

## Prepare useful onboarding

Explain purpose, benefits, boundaries, information practices, standards and where to get help. Keep the first visit focused: members should complete a meaningful task before being asked to build a profile.

Use staged invitations where appropriate. Support people who cannot access the portal or require an alternative format. Give moderators and administrators scenario-based training.

Avoid sending repeated “complete your profile” prompts when the information is optional.

## Run an overlap period deliberately

Keep old and new channels in parallel only for a defined time. Duplicating everything indefinitely teaches members that the new portal is optional and doubles administrator work.

Publish a channel map. Redirect new discussion and maintained information to the portal while keeping necessary notices available through assessed channels. Mark the old platform's closure or archive date clearly.

## Close responsibly

Complete approved exports and records capture. Remove or archive old content according to policy. Revoke administrator access and integrations. Communicate what remains accessible and how member information is handled.

Do not delete a community history impulsively, but do not preserve it forever without purpose. Obtain professional advice where records, legal or contractual obligations apply.

## Review after launch

Look for unanswered searches, recurring support needs, inactive privileged accounts, confusing boundaries and inaccessible content. Ask whether members can accomplish the intended tasks.

Migration is complete when the new place is understood and operated—not on the day data is imported. Use the [community migration guide](/resources/community-migration-guide/) to turn these stages into a working plan.
