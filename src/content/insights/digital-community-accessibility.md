Accessibility determines who can participate in a digital community. It is not a finishing pass or a score attached to the interface. It spans the product, content, authentication, documents, moderation, notifications and support.

Portal Page aims for WCAG 2.2 AA. A standard provides a valuable baseline, but conformance should not be claimed without appropriate testing and evidence.

## Test the community journey

Begin with real tasks: receive an invitation, create or access an account, understand the community, find an announcement, open a document, join a discussion, discover an event, change settings and ask for help.

Test with keyboard, screen reader, zoom, reflow, high contrast and reduced motion. Include disabled people in research and compensate them appropriately. Automated tools find important issues, but they cannot judge whether a complex community task makes sense.

## Make sign-in inclusive

Authentication can be the largest barrier. Labels, instructions, error recovery, time limits and multi-factor methods must be accessible. Provide more than one usable authentication method where policy and risk allow.

Do not make support dependent on the same inaccessible sign-in. A member locked out of the portal needs a reachable alternative.

## Build semantic structure

Use landmarks, headings, lists, buttons and links according to meaning. Preserve a logical focus order and visible focus. Ensure touch targets are usable and controls do not rely on colour or position alone.

At small widths, recompose the experience rather than forcing a desktop table or navigation into horizontal scrolling. Allow text to enlarge without hiding content or controls.

Respect reduced motion. Animation should communicate gently, not block tasks or trigger discomfort.

## Support accessible community content

Administrators and members create the living experience. Provide authoring guidance for headings, descriptive links, image alternatives, captions, plain language and accessible documents.

Do not use image-only notices. A PDF is not automatically accessible because it contains selectable text. For frequent guidance, an accessible web page may be easier to maintain and use than a document download.

Moderators can help correct patterns, but accessibility should not become a reason to punish members who lack expertise. Offer prompts and constructive support.

## Make discussion perceivable

Threads need clear authorship, dates, reply relationships and status. Screen-reader users should be able to navigate without hearing repeated interface clutter. Keyboard users should not encounter traps in editors or menus.

Emoji reactions, colour cues and icons need accessible names or accompanying text. Live updates should be announced appropriately without overwhelming assistive technology.

Moderation and reporting flows require clear labels, confirmation and error recovery. Sensitive content controls should remain operable by everyone.

## Communicate accessible events

Event pages should state time zone, location, participation method, physical and digital access information, costs and contact routes. Registration should work without a mouse and avoid unnecessary time pressure.

Provide captions, transcripts or alternative materials as appropriate. Explain how to request accommodations and avoid requiring a person to disclose more than is necessary.

## Design notifications carefully

Notification text should carry enough meaning outside visual context and avoid revealing sensitive details on a locked screen. Email should use semantic headings and descriptive links. Do not communicate critical information through colour alone.

Give members reasonable control over category and frequency without making the settings difficult to find.

## Operate an accessibility feedback process

Publish an accessibility statement describing scope, known limitations, testing and contact. Give reports an accountable owner, expected response and remediation path.

Track recurring content and product barriers. Include accessibility in regression testing, procurement, release review and administrator training. When a limitation cannot be fixed immediately, provide a practical alternative and communicate honestly.

Accessibility is part of community governance because exclusion changes who can contribute and whose knowledge becomes visible. Read Portal Page's [accessibility approach](/accessibility/) and assess the entire community service—not only the public homepage.
