Communities create value over time. A directory reflects relationships. A document library reflects accumulated work. Discussions contain decisions and experience. Group structures show how the organisation actually operates.

When that value cannot move, the community is trapped by more than inconvenience. It may remain on an unsuitable platform because the cost of reconstructing its memory is too high.

## Export is not automatically portability

An export can be technically complete and practically useless. Text without authorship, dates or group context may lose meaning. Files without descriptions or status become a new pile to sort. Proprietary identifiers can make relationships difficult to rebuild.

Meaningful portability begins by identifying which information must remain usable and what context gives it meaning. The answer differs by purpose. An organisation may need member identities and group relationships for continuity, while a records team needs selected official content in a stable format.

## Portability protects governance

A deliberate exit plan forces the organisation to understand its information. Who is authorised to request an export? Which contributions include third-party rights? What must be retained, anonymised or excluded? How are credentials and secrets removed?

These questions improve current governance even if the organisation never leaves. They expose uncontrolled files, unclear ownership and hidden dependence on individuals.

Portability should not become uncontrolled copying. A private community boundary continues to matter during migration. Exports need protected delivery, authorised recipients and appropriate disposal after use.

## Useful formats preserve context

Prefer documented, widely usable formats where practical. Content may use HTML, Markdown, JSON, CSV or common document formats depending on the information. Attachments should retain human-readable names and relevant metadata.

Relationships matter. A discussion includes posts, replies, authors, groups and dates. A directory includes membership and visibility. An event includes time zone, audience and linked resources. Export documentation should explain how those pieces connect.

No format guarantees easy migration into every other product. Portability means avoiding deliberate obstruction and providing enough structure for a responsible transition.

## Members have portability interests too

An organisation-level export is not the only consideration. Members may have rights to access information about themselves or may reasonably want a copy of contributions. The correct scope depends on law, contract and community context.

A member export must protect other people. A discussion thread can contain many contributors, private details or moderator action. Policies and product behaviour need a careful balance rather than an automatic database dump.

## Service exit is a process

Plan timing, responsibilities, support, verification and deletion. A typical sequence may include a test export, data quality review, protected transfer, import validation, member communication, cutover, final export and service deletion.

Backups and security logs may follow separate limited cycles. The provider should explain them. Restoration processes should not unintentionally return deleted information to active use.

Contracts should describe scope, formats, assistance, costs, notice periods and deletion confirmation. Do not leave critical details to a future support ticket.

## Portability changes vendor incentives

When exit is possible, a provider must earn renewal through value and trust. Artificial lock-in weakens that discipline. A platform may still be difficult to replace because it is deeply useful and integrated; the difficulty should come from genuine organisational complexity, not withheld data.
