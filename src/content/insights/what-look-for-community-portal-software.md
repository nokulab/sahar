The best community portal is not the product with the largest catalogue of features. It is the service an organisation can operate responsibly and members can use to accomplish meaningful tasks.

A feature matrix can support a decision, but it should follow a clear community brief and scenario-based assessment.

## Start with member outcomes

Describe what different members need to do. A resident may need to find a moving guide. An association member may need to discover a chapter event. A government advisory participant may need to read background material and contribute between meetings.

Use those scenarios in demonstrations. Ask the supplier to show the complete task, including invitation, sign-in, accessibility, notification, error and support—not only the ideal screen.

Prioritise requirements as essential, important and optional. Every optional field or feature creates content, privacy and administration work.

## Assess information architecture

Can members distinguish announcements, discussions, documents, events and support? Can the organisation use its own vocabulary without creating a maze? Are groups understandable, and can important public information remain outside the login?

Search should respect permissions and help people recognise authoritative, current material. Look for content ownership, dates and editorial controls rather than treating search as a remedy for disorganisation.

## Test roles and lifecycle

Map the real people who will operate the portal: membership administrators, publishers, moderators, group coordinators, support teams and technical owners. Test whether roles can follow least privilege.

Walk through joining, changing role, temporary access, departure and return. Ask how privileged access is reviewed and what audit information is available. Shared administrator accounts are a warning sign.

## Examine the business model

Understand who pays. Ask whether the provider sells personal information, serves behavioural advertising, builds cross-site profiles or uses private content to train AI models.

Read the contract language about licences, analytics, derived information, product improvement and subprocessors. A privacy-first slogan needs matching terms and product behaviour.

## Verify privacy and portability

Request a data map and processor information. Confirm purposes, locations, access, retention, deletion and member request pathways. Assess whether fields and visibility can be minimised for the community.

Examine exports before signing. Which content, metadata, relationships and configurations are included? Are formats documented? How does the provider support exit, and when are service copies and backups deleted?

## Match security evidence to risk

Ask for current evidence about architecture, secure development, authentication, privileged access, dependency management, logging, backups, incident response, vulnerability management and suppliers.

If a certification exists, check the entity, system, scope and date. Do not infer that a logo covers the proposed service or customer configuration. Government and regulated organisations may require formal assessment against their own policies.

## Include accessibility in the demonstration

Ask which standards and tasks have been tested, which assistive technologies were used, what limitations remain and how remediation is tracked. Test keyboard operation, zoom, reflow, focus, errors, authentication and content authoring.

The organisation will create much of the content, so look for accessible authoring patterns and guidance. Require a process for reporting and resolving barriers after launch.

## Challenge integrations

For each connection, document purpose, fields, direction, authority, frequency, errors and exit. Confirm that the advertised integration supports the required scenario rather than relying on a logo.

An integration expands the security and privacy boundary. Prefer a small number of owned, documented connections to uncontrolled convenience.

## Assess implementation and support

Who is responsible for discovery, configuration, migration, administrator training and launch? How are issues prioritised? Which support channels and response commitments are contractual?

Ask what the customer must provide. A vendor cannot migrate undocumented content, define governance or identify legal obligations on the organisation's behalf.

## Make the decision auditable

Record why the preferred service meets the community purpose, where gaps remain and which claims were verified. Include exit and risk in the comparison, not only purchase price.

Set review dates for assumptions that may change: processors, certification, roadmap dependencies and legal requirements. Contract for material commitments instead of relying on a sales presentation.
