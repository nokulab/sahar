# Portal Page 12-month editorial strategy

## Editorial position

Portal Page should become a trusted source for organisations designing private digital communities. Content should help a real reader make a decision, govern responsibly or explain an issue to colleagues. It should not fabricate product capability, customers, research or authority.

Each article needs a primary intent, accountable author, internal-link destination and review date. High-intent guides lead to a relevant product or contact path without turning every paragraph into a pitch.

## Topic clusters

1. Community portal strategy
2. Privacy, data ownership and responsible AI
3. Government and public-purpose communities
4. Property, strata and resident communities
5. Associations, education and professional communities

7. Migration, procurement and operations

## Twelve-month publishing plan

### Month 1 — Foundations

- Community portal requirements template
- Community portal implementation timeline
- Building the business case for an organisation-owned community
- Community portal terminology: a plain-English glossary

Refresh the platform pillar and connect every launch article to one clear cluster.

### Month 2 — Privacy and ownership

- Data controller, processor and community operator: practical differences
- How to write a community data map
- Data minimisation examples for member profiles
- Questions to ask about AI in private communities

Publish a downloadable, accessible data-mapping worksheet derived from the guide.

### Month 3 — Government

- Government community platform procurement checklist
- Digital community records-management questions for agencies
- Designing accessible invitations for public-purpose groups
- Community portal considerations for grants and volunteer programs

Invite qualified government accessibility and information-management reviewers; do not imply agency endorsement.

### Month 4 — Property and strata

- Resident portal requirements checklist
- Privacy-conscious resident directory design
- Moving guides and building onboarding that residents can use
- Emergency information versus routine building communication

Confirm jurisdiction-specific claims with qualified reviewers or keep guidance general.

### Month 5 — Governance

- How to write community standards people will read
- A moderation escalation matrix for private communities
- How to review administrator access
- Measuring community health without surveillance

Turn governance templates into accessible web resources rather than gated lead magnets.

### Month 6 — Migration

- Community content inventory template
- How to archive an old online community responsibly
- Member communication plan for a platform migration
- Testing permissions before a community launch

Create a migration readiness self-assessment with no required email collection.

### Month 7 — Associations and membership

- Association portal versus association management system
- Designing chapter and branch spaces
- Member directory governance for professional bodies
- Turning member discussion into maintained professional knowledge

### Month 8 — Accessibility

- Accessible community moderation workflows
- Screen-reader testing tasks for community portals
- Making member-created content more accessible
- Accessible event information checklist

Commission testing or expert review before making product-conformance claims.

### Month 9 — Business and employees

- Employee communities without performance surveillance
- Governance for a community of practice
- Customer community portal versus support centre
- Offboarding members when roles change

### Month 10 — Security and procurement

- Security questions for private community platforms
- How to read the scope of a security certification
- Community portal incident-response responsibilities
- Vendor and subprocessor review for community technology

Keep specific technical claims aligned with verified Portal Page evidence.

### Month 11 — Research preparation

- Methodology for a Community Data Ownership survey
- What organisations should measure before migrating a community
- Research ethics for studying private online communities
- Building a transparent research sources and corrections page

Recruit an appropriately diverse sample and publish methodology before results.

### Month 12 — Original research and annual review

- State of Digital Communities report
- Community Data Ownership survey findings
- Privacy Expectations in Online Communities survey
- Annual Portal Page principles review

Publish findings only after legitimate collection, quality review and clear limitations. Release anonymised aggregate tables where privacy and consent permit.

## Original research program

Potential reports include **State of Digital Communities**, **Community Data Ownership survey**, **Government Digital Community report**, **Privacy Expectations in Online Communities survey** and **Residential Community Technology report**.

For each project:

1. Define the research question and publish a methodology plan.
2. Obtain appropriate ethics, privacy and legal review.
3. Recruit a representative sample without claiming representativeness that the method cannot support.
4. Collect the minimum information needed and set retention.
5. Separate research consent from marketing permission.
6. Analyse with documented methods and disclose limitations.
7. Publish useful aggregate findings, source tables where safe, and a corrections route.
8. Never invent responses, sample sizes or findings.

## Quality standard

Every piece should answer a real question, add examples or a reusable framework, link to authoritative sources where claims require them, and avoid unsupported Portal Page product claims. Review legal, security and policy content more frequently than evergreen design guidance.
